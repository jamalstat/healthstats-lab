What do TP, FP, FN and TN mean?
TP: has the disease and the test is positive.   FP: does not have the disease, but the test is positive.
FN: has the disease, but the test is negative.   TN: does not have the disease and the test is negative.
Memory tip: “true” or “false” tells you whether the test result agrees with the reference standard; “positive” or “negative” describes the test result.
Teaching prompt: Before looking at the answers, predict which measures use the diseased group as their denominator and which use the test-positive group.

2. Read the result

Diagnostic accuracy summary

Sensitivity = TP / (TP + FN)

Specificity = TN / (TN + FP)

PPV = TP / (TP + FP)

NPV = TN / (TN + FN)

LR+ = Sensitivity / (1 − Specificity)

LR− = (1 − Sensitivity) / Specificity

Sensitivity and specificity condition on true disease status.

PPV and NPV condition on the observed test result, so they are strongly affected by disease prevalence.

Likelihood ratios summarise how much a test result shifts the odds of disease.

Three steps: convert probability to odds, multiply by the likelihood ratio, then convert the post-test odds back to probability.

From pre-test to post-test probability

Pre-test odds = p / (1 − p)

Post-test odds = Pre-test odds × LR

Post-test probability = Post-test odds / (1 + Post-test odds)

6. Interpretation clinic

Can you interpret the findings correctly?

These questions focus on what diagnostic measures mean in words, not just how they are calculated.

Question 1

A test has sensitivity 90%. Which interpretation is correct?

Question 2

If disease prevalence falls substantially while sensitivity and specificity remain unchanged, what usually happens?

Question 3

What does LR+ primarily tell you?

Question 4

A diagnostic test has specificity 90%. What does this mean?

Question 5

A test has a positive predictive value (PPV) of 75%. Which statement is correct?

Question 6

A test has a negative predictive value (NPV) of 96%. Which statement is correct?

Question 7

A patient's pre-test probability is 20%. After a positive test, the post-test probability is 68.5%. What does 68.5% represent?

Question 8

A patient's pre-test probability is 20%. After a negative test, the post-test probability falls to 3.8%. What is the best interpretation?

For teaching

Suggested classroom sequence

1. Ask students to classify TP, FP, FN and TN from a short clinical vignette. 2. Enter the table and predict sensitivity/specificity before calculating. 3. Move the prevalence slider and discuss why PPV changes. 4. Use LR+ and LR− to connect diagnostic accuracy to clinical probability. 5. Move the biomarker threshold and discuss the sensitivity–specificity trade-off. 6. Finish with the interpretation questions.
